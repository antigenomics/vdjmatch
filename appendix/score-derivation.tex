\begin{abstract}\noindent\small
\texttt{vdjmatch} annotates T-cell receptors with peptide--MHC specificity by searching a labelled
reference (VDJdb) and calibrating every count against a matched background. This appendix is the
reviewable account of the algorithm as it stands. Part~I gives the intuition, the stage-by-stage
flow and a map to the implementation. Part~II specifies the shipped default. Part~III specifies the
opt-in distance-local rank model, its null bound, its Bayesian reading and --- newly ---
the regime in which it degenerates. Part~IV covers reference-defined germline rules.
Part~V states the parameters and why each takes its value. Parts~VI and~VII derive time, space and
accuracy bounds and compare them with recorded observations. Part~VIII lists what remains unproved.
Exact guarantees, conditional results and heuristics are kept apart throughout. The default annotation scorer is unchanged; no experimental route below is a promoted replacement, and no quantity
here is a calibrated biological probability of a true specificity.
\end{abstract}

\part*{I. Intuition and flow}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{I. Intuition and flow}

\section{The problem, in one paragraph}
A T-cell receptor recognises a peptide--MHC complex (pMHC) mainly through its CDR3 loops, and
receptors recognising the same pMHC converge onto similar loops. So: given a query receptor, find
its near neighbours among receptors of \emph{known} specificity, and decide whether that
neighbourhood is more crowded than chance would produce. The second half is the whole difficulty.
Immune repertoires are enormously redundant and some junctions are generically ``popular'';
a raw neighbour count therefore says as much about how ordinary a sequence is as about what it
binds. Every statistic below is a different answer to \emph{crowded relative to what}.

\textbf{Inputs.} A query $q$: a TRA receptor, a TRB receptor, or a genuine linked TRA--TRB pair,
each carrying an anchor-inclusive junction (the Cys104$\ldots$Phe118 string, \emph{not} the
anchor-free IMGT CDR3) and, where available, V and J gene calls. A reference set of VDJdb source
records, each a (junction, V, J, peptide, MHC, study) observation. A background control population:
either generated draws from a recombination model (OLGA) or a real repertoire.

\textbf{Outputs.} Per (query, pMHC target) a ranked candidate row carrying an evidence score, the
supporting counts, a control-calibrated enrichment $p$-value, and the population sizes that produced
them; per query a top-1 call with an explicit status (\code{matched}, \code{ambiguous},
\code{no\_hit}, \code{no\_reference}, \code{invalid\_query}). Distance, database confidence, rank
evidence, $E$-value, conditional model probability and empirical error rate are separate columns and
are never silently fused.

\section{Stages}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Normalise and validate.} Resolve locus, check the junction alphabet, resolve V alleles
  against the bundled templates, apply reference eligibility and any requested exclusions. Query
  identity, locus, V/J and pairing survive every join.
\item \textbf{Index.} Build one \texttt{seqtree} trie per locus over the \emph{distinct} reference
  junctions. Observation multiplicity is retained separately; it must not multiply a search key.
\item \textbf{Search.} One native batch per locus over the distinct query junctions, within a fixed
  edit budget $\theta$. The same batch runs against the control index, giving the cumulative control
  neighbour count $n_c(s)$ at every attained score $s$.
\item \textbf{Score.} Reduce hits to one row per (query, pMHC), weighting each neighbour by its
  similarity and dividing by the local background crowding.
\item \textbf{Calibrate.} Compare the reference neighbour count with the count the background
  predicts, as a Poisson tail.
\item \textbf{Call.} Rank candidates; the top one is the call, exact ties abstain.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Steps 3--5 have opt-in alternatives (Part~III) that replace the edit ball with a TCRdist
geometry and the density with a rank statistic. They are \emph{not} the default.

\part*{II. The shipped default}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{II. The shipped default}

\section{Receptors, targets and populations}
The query $q$ is a TRA, TRB or linked TRA--TRB receptor. Target $X$ includes peptide and MHC
restriction. Its eligible reference set has size $N$; the declared background has size $M$. A
junction includes both conserved anchors. Reference curation precedes every count. Full-junction identity exclusion is benchmark-only;
ordinary annotation retains exact evidence and conflicting labels. The benchmark target gate
requires at least 30 valid source records before puncturing and is not an annotation filter. References and real controls use distinct junction/V keys; generated controls
retain draws. Those different sampling units do not establish a common null distribution.

\section{Search and the NED-v1 density}
Let $\theta=(s_{\max},i_{\max},d_{\max},t_{\max})$ be the edit budget (default $(1,0,0,1)$) and
$B_\theta(q)=\{x:\mathrm{edit}(q,x)\preceq\theta\}$ the ball it defines. For each reference
neighbour $r\in B_\theta(q)$ the engine returns an alignment score $s(q,r)$ under the bundled VDJAM
substitution matrix (integer scale $100$). Let $n_c(s)$ be the number of \emph{control} junctions
within alignment score $s$ of $q$ --- the cumulative control count at that score, obtained from the
same batched search. With $w_V(r)\in\{1\}\cup[0,0.25]$ the soft V-gene weight (Sec.~\ref{sec:par}),
the default per-target evidence is
\[
\boxed{\;S_{\mathrm{NED}}(q,X)=\sum_{r\in B_\theta(q)\cap X}
\frac{w_V(r)\,e^{-s(q,r)/\lambda}}{n_c(s(q,r))+1},\qquad \lambda=400.\;}
\]
The sum runs over distinct voting keys (junction, V, J), so publication multiplicity never
multiplies a vote; record, clonotype, study and cell counts are reported separately.
Reading it: the numerator is similarity, decaying exponentially in alignment cost; the denominator
is how crowded the background is at that same distance, so a neighbour reached only in a region
where the background is also dense is discounted. The $+1$ is a pseudocount making the statistic
finite for a private neighbour, and it is what makes an exact match
\emph{counted rather than automatically accepted}.

Implementation: \code{aggregate.candidates.candidates}. Its \code{background\_mass=True} variant
replaces $w_Ve^{-s/\lambda}$ by one vote per distinct junction and rescales by $M/N$; the
historical variant of \code{match.unified} instead divides by $\max\{N\widehat F^{\rm edit}_q(H),0.01\}$.
All three exist; \textbf{the shipped default is the boxed form above}.

\section{Control-calibrated enrichment}
Let $n_D$ be the number of distinct reference neighbours and $n_C$ the number of distinct control
neighbours in the same ball. Under an exchangeable background the expected number of reference
neighbours is $E=(N/M)\,n_C$ and
\[
p_{\mathrm{enrich}}=\Pr\{\mathrm{Poisson}(E)\ge n_D\}.
\]
The Poisson/Chen--Stein justification, the punctured (self-excluding) null and the choice of $M$ are
derived in the companion \texttt{seqtree} appendix. The quantity is significant only when a receptor
has \emph{more} labelled neighbours than the generative background predicts --- the signature of
antigen-driven convergence rather than of a merely common sequence. It is reported alongside the
ranking and never multiplied into it.

\textbf{First-hit (adaptive) scope.} Rather than fix the radius, \code{evalue.first\_hit} widens to
each query's \emph{nearest} reference hit, up to five total edits with at most two insertions and
two deletions, and evaluates $E$ at that first-hit radius $r^\ast$: $E=(N/M)n_C(r^\ast)$,
$p=\Pr\{\mathrm{Poisson}(E)\ge n_D(r^\ast)\}$. Because the background count grows faster with radius
than the target count (measured in Sec.~\ref{sec:accobs}), the calibration adapts per query with no
hand-tuned scope.

\textbf{Linked pairs.} A paired candidate requires both chains to match the \emph{same} reference
complex. Under the explicitly assumed independent-chain background law, the joint
null factorises, $E_{\alpha\beta}=N\pi_0^\alpha\pi_0^\beta$, while antigen-driven selection makes
the two chains dependent \emph{conditional on an epitope} --- which is exactly the excess the joint
test detects. Exact-pair puncturing removes only the exact/exact corner. The finite-control bound
uses four simultaneous one-sided Clopper--Pearson category bounds with total failure budget
$\delta=10^{-6}$ (\code{evalue.paired.\_joint\_punctured\_results}); $\delta$ is added to the
reported tail, so the result is a bound, not an exact tail. Deduplicated controls do not establish
the IID assumption the bound conditions on.

\textbf{Paired API versus paired benchmark geometry.} The public \code{paired\_candidates}
API searches each chain with its own bounded-edit scope, intersects hits on the same complex and
pMHC, and ranks distinct junction pairs by
$\sum_{(r_\alpha,r_\beta)}\exp[-(s_\alpha+s_\beta)/400]$.
It does not use the summed TCRdist radius below, nor the single-chain control denominator in that
paired similarity sum. Its independent-chain calibration concerns expected counts of common
reference complexes; it does not replace the common-complex support requirement with two unrelated
chain hits. The CLI geometry experiment instead requires $d_\alpha+d_\beta\le D$ to one linked
reference pair. Statements about radius-90 coverage in this appendix concern that experiment.

\part*{III. Distance-local rank evidence (opt-in)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{III. Distance-local rank evidence}

\section{One additive distance}
For residues $a,b$, let $s(a,b)$ be BLOSUM62 and set $c(a,a)=0$,
$c(a,b)=\max(0,\min(4,4-s(a,b)))$ for $a\ne b$. Let $h$ be the junction-length difference, $L$ the
longer length, and $A$ an allowed alignment with one central block of $h$ gaps. The TCRdist-like
distance is
\[
d(q,r)=d_V(q,r)+\min_A\Big\{3\sum_{(i,j)\in A}w_{L,\max(i,j)}\,c(q_i,r_j)+12h\Big\}.
\]
Here $d_V$ sums aligned V-template CDR1/CDR2/CDR2.5 distances, each with weight one; $i,j$ are
zero-based positions in the full junctions. TCRdist3 uses unit interior weights, trimming three
N-terminal and two C-terminal residues. The symmetric comparison trims three at both ends.
Alternatively the shipped empirical profile uses $u_{L,k}=1-\max\{p_V(k),p_J(L-1-k)\}$ and
$w_{L,k}=u_{L,k}/(L^{-1}\sum_t u_{L,t})$. The profiles $p_V,p_J$ are measured same-epitope retention
probabilities indexed from each end, not exact NDN boundaries. Native weights have $0.01$
resolution/minimum; they weight substitutions.

The term $12h$ is a \emph{linear} gap penalty, not an affine opening/extension scheme or a
Smith--Waterman alignment. An affine alternative would be $g(0)=0$,
$g(h)=g_{\rm open}+(h-1)g_{\rm extend}$ for $h>0$; it is a different model. For linked receptors sum
the two chain distances to the \emph{same} reference pair. Do not infer a joint null from
independent marginal control chains.

\section{Historical score and the broad-kernel experiment}
Original vdjmatch sums, over nonexact equal-length neighbours within five substitutions,
\[
S_{\rm old}=\sum_r\frac{a(q,r)e^{-P(q,r)/400}}{\max\{N\widehat F_q^{\rm edit}(H(q,r)),0.01\}}.
\]
Here $a$ is the historical V factor, $P$ the positional BLOSUM Gram penalty, $H$ the substitution
count, and $\widehat F_q^{\rm edit}$ the control edit-ball fraction. Sparse targets additionally use
the original marginal V/J/length log-frequency ratios; historical paired output uses cohort rank
fusion. These contracts remain reproducible and are distinct from the model below.

\textbf{Benchmark baseline scope.} In the AP comparisons below, \code{original\_score} means
the \code{historical-density} CLI output, not NED-v1 from \code{aggregate.candidates} or the
paired API exponential sum. For fewer than 500 unique target junctions its unpaired score is
$\log(S_{\rm old}+10^{-6})$ plus the germline log-ratio; otherwise it is $S_{\rm old}$.
Its paired score sums average cohort ranks of alpha density, beta density and the combined
germline prior, using zero-based ranks. This distinction also applies to the original component
inputs of S8. Historical benchmark AP values therefore do not measure the default match API.

The preceding experimental score was $S_{\rm linear}=(W_X/N)/[(W_B+1/2)/(M+1)]$, where
$W_X=\sum_rK_D(d(q,r))$, $W_B=\sum_bK_D(d(q,b))$ and $K_D(t)=\max(0,1-t/D)$ at fixed radius $D$
(not diameter). It compares broad average proximity. A very close match may still be diluted.
The construction below restores distance-local normalisation; neither experiment is an accepted
replacement default or an established biological TP probability.

\textbf{Background identity matters.} Generative draws measure recombination accessibility; real
controls include biological selection; VDJdb excluding $X$ reflects other annotated specificities
and ascertainment. Keep these questions separate. Point probability $P_{\rm gen}(q)$ is not
neighbourhood probability. Preserve full junctions for identity/Pgen even if terminal matching
weights are zero.

\section{A normalised reciprocal-rank score}
\textbf{Rank and ties.} For reference $r_i$, let $R_i$ be its distance rank among the $M$ controls
plus itself, so $R_i\in\{1,\dots,M+1\}$. Let $H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j$ and $H_0=0$. Define
\[
\boxed{B_X(q)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^Nh_M(R_i),\qquad h_M(r)=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}\,r}.}
\]
This is inverse local background mass with its finite harmonic normalisation, not an exponential
kernel with a fitted scale. In the CLI both populations use integer bins
$\lceil d_{\rm native}/200\rceil$. For $l$ controls in lower bins and $t$ controls in the same bin,
average over the possible tied ranks:
\[
\bar h_{l,t}=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}}\cdot\frac{H_{l+t+1}-H_l}{t+1}.
\]
Do not break ties using target labels or input order.

\textbf{Censoring far evidence.} The fixed radius $D$ determines computation, not a chosen
target-dependent gate. Let $n_D,m_D$ be the reference/control counts inside it. Far references
contribute their conditional null mean
\[
\bar h_{\rm far}=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}}\cdot\frac{H_{M+1}-H_{m_D}}{M-m_D+1},\qquad
B_X=\frac{\sum_{i:d_i\le D}\bar h_i+(N-n_D)\bar h_{\rm far}}N .
\]
No far ordering enters this statistic. No near evidence on either side gives $B_X=1$. A singleton
target is valid. Empty populations are explicitly unavailable; the model posterior then remains its
prior. No clustering minimum, $K$ cap or 20-residue switch is required.

\begin{remark}[numerical form]
$H_{M+1}=\psi(M+2)+\gamma$ and $\bar h$ over an inclusive integer interval $[\ell,u]$ is
$(\psi(u+1)-\psi(\ell))/(u-\ell+1)$; \code{local\_rank.\_harmonic\_interval} evaluates widths
$\le8$ by exact summation instead, because the digamma difference cancels catastrophically for
large, closely spaced ranks. The explicit assignment $B_X\!=\!1$ when $n_D=m_D=0$ is algebraically
redundant --- the general formula already yields $1$ --- and is a float-safety guard.
\end{remark}

\section{Null bound and Bayesian derivation}
\begin{proposition}[expectation-one null]\label{prop:null}
Under $H_0$ --- distance labels exchangeable conditional on the pooled distances and fixed
population sizes --- a randomised reference rank is uniform on $\{1,\dots,M+1\}$, so
$\E_0h_M(R_i)=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{M+1}\sum_{r=1}^{M+1}\frac1r=1$ and hence
$\E_0B_X=1$. Shared controls require no independence between contributions; tie averaging and
genuinely censored far ranks preserve the equality by conditional expectation.
Specifically, condition on the target/control counts in every fixed distance bin. A labelled target
in a bin with $l$ earlier controls and $t$ tied controls has uniform relative rank on
$[l+1,l+t+1]$ after a label-blind tie permutation. Treat the censored suffix as one final bin;
its ranks range from $m_D+1$ to $M+1$. The implemented statistic is the conditional expectation
of the full reciprocal-rank mean given these bin counts, so its unconditional null mean remains one.
No selection on $n_D>0$ is made in this argument. Consequently
\[
\boxed{p_{\rm bound}=\min(1,B_X^{-1}),\qquad \Pr\nolimits_0(p_{\rm bound}\le\alpha)\le\alpha\quad(0<\alpha<1).}
\]
\end{proposition}
This Markov bound is conservative, not the exact tail distribution of $B_X$. A separate
nearest-reference test has $p_{\rm nearest}=\Pr\{K\ge1\}$ for
$K\sim\mathrm{Hypergeom}(N+M,N,m_*+1)$, where $m_*$ counts controls at or below the nearest
reference bin. Upper-bin ties are conservative; censored no-hits receive one. It is not combined
with the full-score bound.

For the \emph{specified rank-tilted alternative} $H_1$, define $dP_1=B_X\,dP_0$. Its mass is one by
Proposition~\ref{prop:null}, so $B_X$ is its Bayes factor. With declared prior $\pi=\Pr(H_1)$,
\[
\boxed{\Pr(H_1\mid\text{rank evidence})=\frac{\pi B_X}{1-\pi+\pi B_X}.}
\]
The CLI default $\pi=1/2$ means equal model odds, not biological specificity prevalence. This
alternative is explicitly chosen; its normalisation is not proof that it describes receptor
recognition. Deduplication, generated-draw multiplicity and ascertainment may violate
exchangeability. Outputs therefore remain conditional model quantities until sampling and
biological calibration are validated. $P$-values are not probability FP, and model posteriors are
not automatically probability TP. Target/route selection requires its own calibration. General
expectation-one evidence: Vovk and Wang, Ann.\ Statist.\ 49:1736--1754 (2021),
\href{https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS2020}{doi:10.1214/20-AOS2020}.

\section{The unsupported-query degeneracy}\label{sec:degen}
The censoring rule is unbiased (Proposition~\ref{prop:null}) but it is not \emph{informative}, and
that distinction decides the statistic's behaviour on sparse targets.

\begin{proposition}[degeneracy at zero support]\label{prop:degen}
If $n_D=0$ --- no reference of target $X$ lies within the radius of $q$ --- then
\[
B_X(q)=\frac{(M+1)\,(H_{M+1}-H_{m_D})}{(M-m_D+1)\,H_{M+1}},
\]
a strictly decreasing function of the control count $m_D$ \emph{alone}. At fixed $M,m_D$ it does not depend on $N$ or any further target-reference distance.
The event $n_D=0$ itself is observed target evidence; a target-specific complement may also change
$M$ and $m_D$. Thus this is loss of within-suffix target ordering, not absence of all target information.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $n_D=0$ the near sum is empty and all $N$ references take $\bar h_{\rm far}$, so
$B_X=\bar h_{\rm far}$, which is the displayed expression. Monotonicity in $m_D$ follows because
$(H_{M+1}-H_{m})/(M-m+1)$ is the mean of $1/r$ over $[m+1,M+1]$, a mean over a shrinking
upper interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[both propositions checked numerically]
Against the shipped \code{local\_rank\_evidence} itself, not a reimplementation.
Proposition~\ref{prop:null}: over $4{,}000$ exchangeable draws at $N=40$, $M=5{,}000$ and $30$ shared
distance bins, $\widehat{\E_0 B_X}=1.00385\pm0.00828$ (s.e.), i.e.\ $0.47$ standard errors from one.
Proposition~\ref{prop:degen}: with $n_D=0$ the code reproduces the closed form to a relative
$10^{-12}$ over five values of $m_D$ spanning $0$ to $M-1$, and is bit-identical across four $N$
at fixed $m_D$: the claimed independence of $N$ is exact, not approximate.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[S8: historical three-block diagnostic, superseded by a corrected trial]\label{rem:threeblock}
A proposed paired route orders three dispositions: a common linked reference inside the radius;
no common reference but at least one supported chain; neither. The saved diagnostic assigns
$77.6\%$ of linked-pair queries and $67.3\%$ of priority queries to the middle block; its reported
$97.4\%$ per-chain-evidence statistic has its own historical denominator.
The implementation sums the two single-chain ranks computed over the \emph{whole task}, then
ranks that sum within the middle block. It does not sum within-block chain ranks.
Its \code{rank01} uses two stable \code{argsort} calls, assigning different ranks to equal scores
according to input order. Consequently the saved changes are historical output, not verified gains:
mean/median AP $0.8205\to0.8285$/$0.8410\to0.8476$ over 625 tasks;
mean AP $0.8201\to0.8297$ over 70 priority tasks, $0.8175\to0.8678$ over 99 paired tasks,
and $0.8261\to0.8738$ over 14 priority paired tasks (76 paired wins, 23 losses).
The recorded one-chain increment is $0.0241$, versus $0.0504$ for the two-chain diagnostic.
These historical figures are not results of the corrected tie-preserving implementation.
Its fresh curated-reference trial is reported in Section~\ref{sec:s8} and supersedes them for evaluating S8.
\end{remark}

\section{S8: implemented cohort-relative paired ordering}\label{sec:s8}
The opt-in \code{paired-evidence-order} CLI implements the corrected reduction in
\code{aggregate.paired\_order.paired\_evidence\_order}. It performs no sequence search and does
not change the default scorer. The intended input uses common-pair and marginal support counts
from the same fixed-radius geometry ($D=90$), the fixed-radius paired rank score, and original
paired/alpha/beta scores. The reduction validates their shape and values; it does not establish
the provenance or radius of an externally supplied table.

Let $g$ identify the complete cohort, species and full pMHC (peptide, both MHC fields and MHC
class). Each query identifier is unique within $g$; absent MHC fields remain null categories.
Rows carry explicit identifiers: pairing and cross-file linkage must be established by those
identities, never by row position. For query $q$, let $a_q$ indicate that both query chains are
present and valid, and let $c_{qP},c_{q\alpha},c_{q\beta}$ be nonnegative integer neighbour counts.
Unavailable counts are null, treated as zero only for routing and retained as null in output.
Define its block
\[
b_q=\begin{cases}
1,&a_q\text{ and }c_{qP}>0,\\
2,&a_q\text{ and }c_{qP}=0\text{ and }(c_{q\alpha}>0\text{ or }c_{q\beta}>0),\\
3,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Block 1 counts a \emph{common linked reference pair}, not two independent chain hits.
Missing/invalid pairs always enter block 3, with status \code{paired\_evidence\_unavailable};
valid pairs without support have status \code{no\_reference\_neighbour}.

For a finite multiset $A$ of scores define the ascending average rank
\[
\mathcal R_A(x)=\#\{y\in A:y<x\}+\frac{\#\{y\in A:y=x\}+1}{2}.
\]
Thus larger scores have larger ranks and ties share exactly one rank. Write $P_q$ for the
fixed-radius paired rank score, $F_q$ for original paired fallback evidence, and
$A_q,B_q$ for original alpha/beta evidence. Null scores behave as $-\infty$, tying below all
finite scores; numeric infinities and NaNs are rejected. Common-pair support requires finite
$P_q$. An observed chain with no hit remains available even when its score is null.
Within each $(g,b)$ population form
\[
T_q=\begin{cases}
P_q,&b_q=1,\\
\mathcal R_{\{A_u:u\in(g,2)\}}(A_q)+
\mathcal R_{\{B_u:u\in(g,2)\}}(B_q),&b_q=2,\\
F_q,&b_q=3.
\end{cases}
\]
With $n_{gb}$ its block size and $r_q=\mathcal R_{\{T_u:u\in(g,b)\}}(T_q)$, output
\[
S_q=3-b_q+\frac{r_q}{n_{gb_q}+1}.
\]
Since $1\le r_q\le n_{gb_q}$, the score lies strictly in $(2,3)$, $(1,2)$ or $(0,1)$ for
blocks 1, 2 or 3. This proves strict block separation without arbitrary large offsets.
Equal component scores and equal rank sums remain tied; permuting input rows only permutes outputs.
The implementation preserves input columns and row order, normalises count columns to nullable
UInt32, and appends the block, status, within-block rank and evidence score.

Ranks use the \emph{complete} declared population, not independently processed chunks. Changing
cohort membership can change the middle-block order. These are empirical cohort/full-pMHC-relative
scores, not comparable across populations; they are not a winning-label confidence, P-value,
E-value, posterior or biological specificity probability. The algorithm consumes no outcome labels.
A constant number of grouped sorts gives $O(\sum_g n_g\log n_g)$ time and $O(Q)$ extra space,
where $n_g$ is the number of rows in population $g$ and $Q=\sum_g n_g$; no distance matrix is built.
\textbf{Fresh curated-reference result (2026-10-11).} Rebuilding all 20 raw views took
$1208.494641$ seconds and $4.273143808$ GB sampled peak process-tree RSS
($4{,}273{,}143{,}808$ bytes). The 615,224 raw appearances yielded 620 eligible tasks and
485,970 evaluated appearances. All 19,677,866 checked baseline cells were exact; all 522
single-chain tasks (412,529 appearances) retained their original scores.
Across 620 tasks, mean AP rose from $0.821989$ for the historical-density baseline to $0.829362$
for S8; median AP rose from $0.843176$ to $0.851827$. Over 98 paired tasks (73,441 appearances),
S8 mean/median AP was $0.865949/0.890249$, versus $0.819304/0.860469$ for the historical baseline
and $0.825800/0.857693$ for conditional rank. S8 won/lost 79/19 paired tasks against the
historical baseline and 65/33 against conditional rank, with no ties in either comparison.
The priority summary, treating equivalent HLA spellings as aliases, includes all eight requested
targets and 14 paired tasks (35,190 appearances). Mean/median AP is $0.868660/0.875889$ for the
historical baseline, $0.895001/0.927630$ for conditional rank and $0.872779/0.881820$ for S8.
Thus S8 improves the historical baseline but does not retain the conditional-rank priority gain.
These aggregate gains do not establish nonregression: in the full-reference peptide-level NLV
paired task (20,034 appearances, 10,137 positive and 9,897 negative), AP fell from conditional
rank's $0.729757$ to S8's $0.622556$ (historical baseline $0.609151$).
S8 is therefore not promoted; the default annotation API and the prior conditional-rank route
remain unchanged. The fresh result establishes the corrected algorithm's measured behaviour,
not the historical prototype's claims or the complete manuscript-workflow resource gate.

\begin{remark}[scope of the tested reformulations]\label{rem:exhausted}
Three recorded reformulations did not establish an accepted improvement. Dropping the
far-imputation term from the ranking is mathematically well motivated --- a synthetic sweep of the
far background moves $B_X$ by $0.215$ while the near mass is invariant to $1.4\times10^{-14}$ ---
yet it changes pooled average precision by $+0.0001$. Averaging over supported references instead of
all of them is worse by $0.0316$ pooled. Tie-breaking is vacuous: $B_X$ already resolves to $1.00$
distinct values per query at $N=30$. Routing remains a mechanism to test; input-order tie breaking in the saved routing scripts
prevents interpreting their AP changes as validated improvements.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what this costs]\label{rem:degencost}
Within a one-versus-all task, every query with $n_D=0$ is therefore ranked purely by how crowded its
\emph{background} neighbourhood is --- a background-crowding summary with no further ordering of the censored target distances. The damage scales with the fraction of such queries. Measured on the
recorded all-task complement run, the median supported fraction is $0.817$ for single chains but
only $0.173$ for linked pairs, because the paired route applies the same numeric radius to the
\emph{sum} $d_\alpha+d_\beta$, which is a roughly twofold tighter per-chain constraint. The
consequences are quantified in Sec.~\ref{sec:accobs}. This is a property of the censoring
imputation, not a defect of the rank construction, and it is an open design question
(Part~VIII), not a fixed parameter choice.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the paired ball is right, and tightness is why]\label{rem:ball}
Linked-pair support requires a \emph{common} reference pair inside the radius: $d_\alpha+d_\beta\le
D$ to the same pair. The natural loosening --- asking only that each chain independently have some
reference neighbour, a diagnostic relaxation, not the support rule of \code{paired\_candidates} ---
provides little separation in this recorded comparison. At the same $D=90$, measured over the fourteen priority linked-pair tasks, the
common-pair criterion covers $0.593$ of positives against $0.054$ of negatives (a $11.1\times$
purity ratio) while the independent criterion covers $0.974$ against $0.954$ ($1.0\times$); over all
ninety-nine linked-pair tasks, $32.8\times$ against $1.2\times$. The tightness of the summed
constraint is therefore not an accident of implementation --- it supplies substantial discrimination in this diagnostic, and the low coverage it causes is better read as a shortage of linked
reference pairs than as a radius that needs widening.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the repair is the disposition, not the statistic]\label{rem:disposition}
Two corrections are easily confused. Removing unsupported \emph{queries} from a comparison is not
the same operation as removing censored \emph{references} from the statistic, and only the first is
beneficial. Measured over the recorded all-task run: leaving $B_X$ unchanged and merely ranking
$n_D=0$ queries below every supported query --- ordering them among themselves by the existing
density --- raises mean average precision from $0.8179$ to $0.8460$ over all $99$ linked-pair tasks
($69$ wins, $23$ losses, $7$ ties) and from $0.8272$ to $0.8776$ over the $14$ priority linked-pair
tasks ($12$/$2$). Recomputing $B_X$ over supported references only, with the same block order,
instead \emph{loses} $0.0316$ mean average precision pooled and $0.0694$ on priority single chains,
because it discards censored far-reference mass that is genuinely informative once most references
are far. The ablation attributes the gain to the block order, not to conditioning the statistic.
Both are historical diagnostics, not promoted gains: their ordinal rank transforms also split
score ties by input order and require tie-safe revalidation.
\end{remark}

\section{Nearest-conditioned rank}\label{sec:ncr}
\code{local\_rank.nearest\_conditioned\_rank} refines exactly the $n_D=0$ branch. Let $k$ be the
number of controls at or below the nearest reference distance. The nearest reference then has
control rank exactly $k+1$; the remaining $N-1$ references have ranks in $[k+1,M+1]$, and the
refinement assigns each the mean reciprocal rank over that interval:
\[
B_X^{\rm cond}=\frac{M+1}{N\,H_{M+1}}\left[\frac1{k+1}+(N-1)\cdot\frac{H_{M+1}-H_{k}}{M-k+1}\right].
\]
References with radius support are untouched. Counting all control ties at the nearest distance is
conservative.

\begin{remark}[nearest-rank null and output ownership]\label{rem:ncrobligation}
Write $K$ for the number of controls before the first target in a label-blind random tie ordering.
Conditional on $K=k$, the suffix is a uniform permutation of $N-1$ target labels and $M-k$
control labels. Each remaining \emph{labelled} target therefore has uniform relative rank on
$[k+1,M+1]$, although the ordered target ranks are dependent. This proves the displayed
conditional mean; dependence does not invalidate linearity of expectation.
With fixed pooled distances, the event of no target inside the fixed radius is measurable from
$K$ and the pooled radius-prefix size. Combining the original supported branch with this
conditional mean on the unsupported branch preserves null expectation one without ties.
The implemented upper-tie count $k$ is at least $K$. Both reciprocal terms decrease in $k$,
so the tied refinement has null expectation at most one and admits a conservative reciprocal
Markov bound under the same exchangeability model. A subunit-mean tied statistic is not a
normalised likelihood ratio without further normalisation.
The CLI writes this score to \code{conditional\_rank\_bayes\_factor}; it leaves
\code{rank\_bayes\_factor}, \code{p\_rank\_bound} and \code{posterior\_rank\_signal} attached to
the original fixed-radius statistic. It does not publish a transferred bound or posterior for the
refinement. The earlier review's objection based on dependence of ordered ranks was incorrect.
\end{remark}

\part*{IV. Reference-supported V rules and exhaustive CDR3 rank}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{IV. Reference-supported V rules}

This opt-in ranking diagnostic keeps germline usage separate from sequence geometry. Let $g$ be the
query V gene, or its linked alpha/beta V-gene tuple. Let $N,M$ be the target and other-peptide
population sizes, and $n_g,m_g$ their counts in category $g$, after benchmark-only full-junction
puncturing. Populations retain distinct full-junction/resolved-V-allele keys. Let $K$ be the number
of observed population V categories plus one unseen category. Define
\[
p_g=\frac{n_g+m_g+1/2}{N+M+K/2},\qquad
L_g=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(n_g+p_g)/(N+1)}{(m_g+p_g)/(M+1)},&n_gm_g>0,\\[4pt] 1,&n_gm_g=0.\end{cases}
\]
Missing category support on either side contributes no V evidence. For supported categories,
calculate the multi-reference statistic $B_X$ using only same-category target/control CDR3 distances
and populations $n_g,m_g$; otherwise use CDR3 distances across all categories with populations
$N,M$. Both routes have zero V-loop cost and trim three full-junction residues at each end. Linked
distances compare both query chains with the same reference pair. No reference is censored:
distances are multiples of three and are bounded by twelve times the sum of maximum full-junction
lengths over the chain axes. The final distance-bin counts must equal the punctured population
counts. Write this exhaustive rank statistic as $B_g$; the ranking score is $S_g=L_gB_g$.

For a motif family, let $T_q$ contain distinct query tokens; linked queries use token tuples from
their two chains. Let $a_t,b_t$ count same-category target/control receptors containing token $t$,
with paired presence required within the same linked receptor. For a supported category and nonempty
$T_q$,
\[
e_q=\frac1{|T_q|}\sum_{t\in T_q}\max\!\left(0,\frac{a_t}{n_g}-\frac{b_t}{m_g}\right),\qquad
S_{g,T}=S_g(1+e_q),\quad 0\le e_q\le1;
\]
otherwise $e_q=0$. Exact three-residue, IMGT-class three-residue, internal-wildcard four-residue and
class-wildcard four-residue families are separate predefined diagnostics. Reference populations
define enrichment; query tokens only look up those rules. The bounded motif factor is correlated
sequence evidence, not an independent likelihood. V is counted once, and no J or length marginal
likelihood is multiplied in. Neither $S_g$ nor $S_{g,T}$ inherits the rank model's null bound or
posterior. Biological call calibration and selected-label error remain separate tasks.

\begin{remark}[prior-only categories]
If $n_g=m_g=0$ the shared prior still yields $L_g\to(M+1)/(N+1)$ under unequal population sizes even
though no germline rule was observed. That is a model-prior artefact, not evidence, and
reference-defined activation must not credit it.
\end{remark}

\section{Reconstruction, invariants and termination}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item Preserve source records and reasons for exclusion; derive eligible search/voting keys.
      Validate anchors, locus and pairing before constructing the index.
\item Batch all distinct queries for each locus under the declared geometry; apply the same
      geometry and bin cutoffs to reference and background. Restore every query identity after search.
\item For each target form cumulative integer counts $n_j,m_j$ at increasing cutoffs $d_j$.
      Require $0\le n_j\le N$, $0\le m_j\le M$ and monotonicity. Query puncturing, when requested,
      modifies both counts and populations, using full-junction identity.
\item Difference cumulative counts to obtain disjoint shells; multiply each target shell count
      by its tie-averaged harmonic contribution. Add the far-shell contribution and divide by $N$.
      For the optional nearest refinement update only the separate conditional-score column.
\item Retain unavailable populations and no hits explicitly. Rank within the declared query/target
      task, preserving exact score ties; report calibration columns with their owning statistic.
\end{enumerate}
Each record and finite search state is consumed a bounded number of times. The edit budget and
finite trie bound traversal; the geometry route has finitely many reference pairs and admissible
single-gap positions. These facts establish termination. Shells partition all $N$ references,
including the far shell, proving equivalence between the batched reduction and the stated sum.
This is algorithmic correctness conditional on the native geometry/search contract, not a theorem
that distance identifies antigen recognition. Whole-population permutation tests of ties and query
identity are required before any proposed cohort-rank route becomes an implementation.

\textbf{Methodological sources.} Knuth, \emph{The Art of Computer Programming}, Volume 3,
\emph{Sorting and Searching}, second edition (1998), is the reference for separating search cost
from sorting/reduction cost; publication details are verified on
\href{https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html}{the author's volume catalogue}.
Heng Li's \emph{Minimap2: versatile pairwise alignment for nucleotide sequences},
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1708.01492v3}{arXiv:1708.01492v3 (2017)},
Sections 6.1.1 and 6.2.3, separates the chaining objective from bounded-search and Z-drop
heuristics. The relevant lesson here is to state a computational shortcut and its completeness
limits separately from the score. No minimap2 nucleotide model or accuracy result is transferred
to TCR matching; these are methodological references, not proofs of vdjmatch's null model.

\part*{VI. Performance bounds}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{VI. Performance bounds}
\label{sec:perf}

\textbf{Model and sizes.} Unit-cost word RAM. $Q$ distinct query junctions; $N_{\rm seq}$ distinct
reference junctions and $N_{\rm rec}\ge N_{\rm seq}$ reference observation records for the locus;
$M$ control junctions; $L$ the maximum junction length; $|\Sigma|=20$; edit budget $t=t_{\max}$;
$R_D,R_C$ the numbers of returned reference and control hits (the \emph{output} sizes).

\textbf{Indexing.} Trie construction over distinct junctions is $O((N_{\rm seq}+M)L)$ time and
space. It is paid once per reference/control, not per query.

\textbf{Search.} Bounded-edit trie traversal enumerates the $t$-neighbourhood of the query rather
than scanning the reference. The number of distinct strings within edit distance $t$ of a length-$L$
string is $O\!\big((L|\Sigma|)^t\big)$, and each is resolved by an $O(L)$ walk, so per query the
work is
\[
O\!\big(L^{\,t+1}|\Sigma|^{t}\big)+O(\text{hits}),\qquad\text{independent of } N_{\rm seq}\text{ and } M .
\]
\emph{This is the central performance property:} at the default $t=1$ the search cost does not grow
with the size of VDJdb. The whole-batch bound is
$O\!\big(Q\,L^{t+1}|\Sigma|^{t}+R_D+R_C\big)$. The enumeration bound is worst-case and loose --- the
trie prunes --- so the \emph{measured} growth factor per extra edit is the operative number
(Table~\ref{tab:perf}).

\textbf{Reduction.} Hits expand from search keys to observation records with factor
$\rho=N_{\rm rec}/N_{\rm seq}$ (measured: $1.50$ TRB, $1.58$ TRA on the 2026-06-11 human release),
then group by (query, pMHC): $O(|H|\log|H|)$ with $|H|=\sum_{h\in H_{\rm search}}m(h)$, where $m(h)$ is the matched key's record multiplicity.

\textbf{Space.} $O((N_{\rm seq}+M)L)$ for indices plus $O(|H|)$ for the hit table. The hit table,
not the index, is the memory risk, and it is bounded by the output size, which is why a wide scope
is a memory decision and not only a time decision.

\textbf{Geometry route.} \code{distance\_matrix} is dense $\Theta(QN_{\rm seq})$ int32 and is
explicitly capped at 64\,MiB per output array. Oversized calls raise an error; callers must batch at most
$\lfloor 64\,\text{MiB}/(4N_{\rm seq})\rfloor$ queries. The counting route
(\code{total\_distance\_count\_batch}) avoids the dense matrix: a conservative exhaustive bound is
$O(QN_{\rm seq}L^2)$ when up to $O(L)$ allowed gap positions each inspect $O(L)$ residues but output is only $O(Q\cdot|\text{cutoffs}|)$. The rank route is therefore
\emph{linear in reference size per query}, unlike the default search --- this is the dominant cost
asymmetry between the two routes and the reason the geometry arms need a resource gate.

\textbf{Parallelism.} One layer only: the single native search batch, threaded internally. No outer
pool, no worker per query.

\part*{VII. Accuracy: what can be bounded, and what is observed}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{VII. Accuracy bounds and observations}
\label{sec:accobs}

\textbf{What is exactly guaranteed.} The principal fixed-radius guarantee is:
Proposition~\ref{prop:null} gives $\Pr_0(p_{\rm bound}\le\alpha)\le\alpha$, a valid but conservative
type-I bound for the fixed-radius statistic over the full exchangeable population. Conditioning the
population on observed support requires another argument; the theorem does not assert it. The nearest-refinement result in
Remark~\ref{rem:ncrobligation} has the same sampling assumption; neither bounds biological accuracy.

\textbf{What is conditional.} The Poisson enrichment tail is valid to Chen--Stein error under the
background model; the punctured paired bound is valid up to its declared $\delta=10^{-6}$ under IID
category sampling and independent background chains. Both assumptions are stated, neither is
verified by the data: deduplicated controls and generated-draw multiplicity do not establish
exchangeability, and this is the appendix's principal unverified premise.

\textbf{Why no useful distribution-free top-1 bound follows.} Distance does not determine a
specificity label. A useful prediction bound needs assumptions linking the labelled reference and
future queries, beyond completeness of the search. The measured macro neighbour purity (mean over
epitopes, human TRB) is $0.49,0.24,0.15,0.10,0.07$ at edit distances $1..5$, with reported
$56\times\!\to\!2.5\times$ enrichment over chance. These are descriptive pairwise proportions,
not a Bayes ceiling on an arbitrary neighbourhood classifier: multiple neighbours, their labels,
germline features and cohort structure can carry information absent from a random neighbour.
The result motivates local background correction but does not bound attainable AP or top-1 accuracy.

\textbf{A derived consequence, and its measurement.} The first-hit calibration rests on the claim
that the background ball fills faster than the target ball. That is checkable. At $N/M=0.282$ the
measured enrichment $n_D/E$ falls from $25.7\times$ at $t=1$ ($9{,}732$ reference vs $1{,}342$
control hits) to $3.6\times$ at $t=3$ ($324{,}218$ vs $319{,}946$): the control count grows
$238\times$ while the reference count grows $33\times$. The mechanism the adaptive scope relies on
is therefore real and quantified.

are ranked by background crowding alone, so accuracy should degrade with target support. It does,

Case-level confirmation at radius $D=90$, $M\approx1.1\times10^5$ (human TRB): for ALWALPHAA
($N=30$) the median query has one reference inside $D$ and $12/31$ positives and $15/31$ negatives
have none; the Bayes-factor medians are $0.4112$ (positives) and $0.4174$ (negatives) --- closely spaced descriptive medians (no test claimed), AP $0.8841\to0.4893$, i.e.\ chance for a balanced task. For CINGVCWTV ($N=386$)
the medians are $1.4397$ vs $0.5612$; for GILGFVFTL ($N=6{,}686$), $2.8178$ vs $0.7514$.
Restricting each task to its supported subpopulation ($n_D\ge1$, the support boundary of the
statistic, not a fitted threshold) removes about half the deficit at low support, flips the sign at
$\ge300$ positives, and removes the \emph{entire} linked-pair deficit ($-0.1408\to+0.0005$ over 86
paired tasks). Prediction and observation agree; the agreement is not itself proof of the
mechanism, and does not exclude other mechanisms such as geometry, reference ascertainment or task composition.

\textbf{Where the linked-pair metric is actually decided.} On the eight priority targets, when a
linked reference pair lies inside the radius both the historical-density benchmark baseline and the rank arm reach AP
$0.9984$--$1.0000$ on eleven of fourteen tasks. The metric is therefore decided on the $52$--$92\%$
of queries that have no supported linked reference. For linked-pair annotation of well-populated
targets the binding constraint is \textbf{coverage, not the score}. Support is also an unusually
pure indicator in itself: over the fourteen priority linked-pair tasks at $D=90$ a linked reference
pair lies inside the radius for $0.593$ of positives against $0.057$ of negatives, a $10.4\times$
ratio of support proportions. This motivates the support-first routing hypothesis
(Remark~\ref{rem:disposition}); the historical routing diagnostic split score ties by input order,
so its apparent gains are not validated gains of the corrected implementation. The median
single-chain supported fraction is $0.993$. The corrected S8 trial in Section~\ref{sec:s8} reports aggregate gains with remaining major task losses; no promotion follows. Widening the radius trades that purity for reach monotonically: $0.725$ against $0.173$
($4.2\times$) at $D=120$, $0.850$ against $0.425$ ($2.0\times$) at $D=150$, and by $D=240$ both
populations are fully covered and the indicator carries nothing. The separation is broad and nearly
flat from $D=90$ to $D=150$ while the ratio falls steeply, so this is a genuine trade-off, not an
optimum to be read off. (The supported subpopulation is
easier by construction, so these are not deployable AP values; the decomposition --- which
population decides the metric --- is the result.)

\begin{remark}[acceptance geometry]
Because the per-task delta is monotone in support and the eight priority targets all lie above the
crossover, a pooled all-task mean and a priority-target mean can disagree in \emph{sign} for the
same arm. They do: over the 70 priority tasks the conditional-rank arm gains $+0.0213$ mean AP
($+0.0697$ on the 14 linked-pair tasks, worst-case AP $0.5082\to0.7298$), while over all 620 tasks
it loses $-0.0391$. Both must be reported under the acceptance rule: priority improvements do not override
mean/median nonregression and review of major task losses.
\end{remark}

\part*{VIII. Open obligations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{VIII. Open obligations}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Exchangeability is assumed, not shown.} Reference deduplication, generated-draw
  multiplicity and study ascertainment all threaten it. Every $p$-value and posterior here is
  conditional on it.
\item \textbf{Nearest-conditioned score ownership.} Its exchangeable null is derived in
  Remark~\ref{rem:ncrobligation}; the existing bound/posterior columns still refer to the original
  statistic. Neither result transfers through further germline multiplication or route selection.
\item \textbf{Validate routing of the $n_D=0$ branch.} Proposition~\ref{prop:degen} establishes
  loss of within-suffix target-distance ordering at fixed background counts, not absence of all
  target information. Restricting the statistic to supported references changes its normalisation
  and does not inherit Proposition~\ref{prop:null}. The historical routing and supported-only
  diagnostics are retained in Remark~\ref{rem:disposition}, with their tie-splitting limitation.
  The corrected S8 routing hypothesis is implemented (Section~\ref{sec:s8}); its fresh curated
  evaluation improves aggregate AP but retains major task losses, so it is not promoted. Its population-relative ordering has no transferred null guarantee.
\item \textbf{The paired radius and the single-chain radius are not the same quantity} and currently
  share a numeral (Remark~\ref{rem:degencost}).
\item \textbf{No biological calibration.} $p_{\rm bound}$ is not $\Pr(\text{FP})$ and the posterior
  is not $\Pr(\text{TP})$. The empirical any-call operating point is a separate deployment artifact.
\item \textbf{The low-support regime is unexplained.} Below roughly $100$ positives every
  geometry arm tested, including the unmodified native TCRdist3 comparator, stays behind the
  original density scorer, and three rounds of reformulating the rank statistic did not move it.
  That it is common to every geometry suggests a property of the regime rather than of any one
  construction, but no mechanism has been established and none should be asserted.
\item \textbf{Occam.} Twelve scoring routes are maintained in parallel and every candidate has been
  rejected for promotion; the default is unchanged. Each should be promoted or retired behind its
  recorded run artifacts.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Default annotation and the historical benchmark baseline remain unchanged; M3 stays deferred.
