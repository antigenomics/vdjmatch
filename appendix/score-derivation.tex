\begin{center}
{\Large A common neighbourhood geometry for reference evidence and background}\par
{\small Implementation derivation: experimental single-chain matched-PSSM score}
\end{center}
\textbf{Populations.}
A query $q$ is a full junction including conserved anchors.
$R_X$ contains compatible reference junction representatives for epitope $X$;
$B$ contains unique full control junctions.
Set $N_X=|R_X|$, $M=|B|$.
References retain the first source-order V call per junction.
Exact junction identity is excluded symmetrically, while population denominators
are retained; unusable references remain in $N_X$ and are reported separately.
Controls are the declared generation/repertoire population, not other VDJdb epitopes.
No assumption of naive-cell provenance follows from the score.

\textbf{Geometry.}
Let $s(a,b)$ be BLOSUM62 and
$\Delta(a,b)=s(a,a)+s(b,b)-2s(a,b)$ its native nonnegative integer penalty.
Use $w_{L,j}=\max\{1,\operatorname{round}(100\omega_{L,j})\}$, where
$\omega$ is the shipped end-anchored empirical significance profile, mean one.
For aligned residue columns $A(q,r)$, $L=\max(\ell_q,\ell_r)$ and
$h=|\ell_q-\ell_r|$,
\begin{equation}
d(q,r)=\sum_{(i,j)\in A(q,r)}w_{L,\max(i,j)}\Delta(q_i,r_j)+g(h),
\quad g(0)=0,\quad g(h)=2800+1400(h-1)\ (h>0).
\end{equation}
The single length-difference block lies after six matched prefix residues,
clamped to the shorter junction length (Cys index zero).
Equal lengths have no gap. This is fixed-gap, not general local alignment.
The cutoff $D=7000$ excludes $h>4$.
The separate historical V factor $a(q,r)$ is one for identical allele-stripped
genes, otherwise $0.25\,\mathrm{vsim}(V_q,V_r)$.
It is not a V-loop likelihood or a CDR1/CDR2/CDR2.5 distance.

\textbf{One score.}
Define the nonexact empirical background ball mass and exponential kernel:
\begin{equation}
\F(t)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{b\in B}\ind\{b\ne q,\ d(q,b)\le t\},
\qquad K_\tau(t)=e^{-t/\tau}\ind\{t\le D\}.
\end{equation}
The implemented statistic is
\begin{equation}\boxed{
S_X(q;\tau)=\sum_{r\in R_X,\ r\ne q}
\frac{a(q,r)K_\tau(d(q,r))}{\max\{N_X\F(d(q,r)),\varepsilon\}},
\quad \varepsilon=0.01,\quad \tau=400\ \text{by default}.}
\end{equation}
Writing $n_X^a(t)=\sum_{r\ne q}a(q,r)\ind\{d(q,r)\le t\}$ gives the compact form
\begin{equation}
S_X=\int_{[0,D]}\frac{e^{-t/\tau}}{\max\{N_X\F(t),\varepsilon\}}\,dn_X^a(t).
\end{equation}
Thus close reference evidence is weighted by the expected unweighted count
under the identical neighbourhood predicate. No length switch or top-$K$ cap
is needed in this expression. A single-distance shell with unit V weights
reduces to $e^{-t/\tau}n_X(t)/\max\{N_X\F(t),\varepsilon\}$.

\textbf{Derivation and statistical meaning.}
For independent reference draws from the control distribution, a fixed-ball
count has mean $N_XF_q(t)$ and binomial variance $N_XF_q(t)(1-F_q(t))$.
Rare balls allow a Poisson approximation.
This establishes the expected-count denominator, not a P-value for the sum.
V factors occur only in the numerator: the denominator is not an expected
V-weighted count. Finite-control uncertainty is not integrated into $S_X$.
With known $F_q$ and unit V weights,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_0[S_X]=N_X\int_{[0,D]}
\frac{e^{-t/\tau}}{\max\{N_XF_q(t),\varepsilon\}}\,dF_q(t)\ne 1
\quad\text{in general}.
\end{equation}
Without the floor, the continuous part is
$\int e^{-t/\tau}\,d\log F_q(t)$, potentially divergent near zero mass.
The floor bounds each contribution by $a(q,r)/\varepsilon$.
It is inherited regularisation, not exact finite-control calibration.

\begin{center}{\large Historical correspondence and limiting cases}\end{center}
\textbf{Original formula.}
The original equal-length, at-most-five-substitution density is
\begin{equation}
S_X^{\mathrm{old}}(q)=
\sum_{\substack{r\ne q,\ \ell_r=\ell_q\\ h(q,r)\le5}}
\frac{a(q,r)e^{-p(q,r)/400}}
{\max\{N_X\widehat F_q^{\mathrm{edit}}(h(q,r)),0.01\}}.
\end{equation}
Here $p(q,r)$ is the positional BLOSUM penalty and $h(q,r)$ the substitution
count. The denominator counts the ordinary unit-edit ball, allowing the
declared historical insertion/deletion scope for controls.
The numerator and denominator therefore use different geometries.
This is deliberate reproduction of the earlier implementation.

Matched mode uses the same positional distance in the kernel and background
CDF and rescores every accepted nonexact neighbour within $D$.
It does not add its score to the old density.
The old and new scores agree only when accepted edges, penalties, V factors
and background masses agree.
Equal length alone does not imply this equality.
The separate additive historical-PSSM experiment retains the old density
and adds only edges outside the original substitution ball.

\textbf{Full output score.}
When the original germline prior is requested, its fusion is preserved:
\begin{equation}
T_X(q)=\begin{cases}
\log(S_X(q)+\eta)+G_X(q),&N_X<500,\\
S_X(q),&N_X\ge500,
\end{cases}\qquad\eta=10^{-6}.
\end{equation}
Without that option, the output is $S_X$.
The original prior is a sum over features $f$ (V/J/length for beta,
V/J for alpha) with category counts $c_{X,f},c_{B_G,f}$ and
raw prior-background size $M_G$:
\begin{equation}
G_X(q)=\sum_f\log\left[
\frac{(c_{X,f}(f(q))+0.5)/(N_X+30)}
     {(c_{B_G,f}(f(q))+0.5)/(M_G+30)}\right].
\end{equation}
This prior population $B_G$ is separate from the unique-junction control $B$;
raw multiplicities are retained. The constants and 500-reference switch are
historical conventions, not consequences of the kernel derivation.
Original alpha reproduction deliberately uses the declared original beta
prior background. Neither that choice nor paired cohort-rank fusion defines
a new probabilistic model.

\textbf{Limiting cases.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item No accepted reference neighbours gives $S_X=0$.
\item As $\tau\to0$, positive-penalty edges vanish; nonexact zero-penalty edges,
if present, retain their contribution.
For positive nonexact penalties, the sparse-reference output tends to
$\log\eta+G_X$: changing $\tau$ changes density/prior balance, so better
density ranking need not improve fused ranking.
\item As $\tau\to\infty$, attenuation disappears within $D$: this remains a
sum of inverse-background-mass contributions, not one ball-count ratio.
\item Uniform position weights and forbidden gaps give weighted-BLOSUM
Hamming geometry; unit substitution penalties give ordinary Hamming geometry.
\item More controls at fixed distribution stabilise $\widehat F_q$; they do
not multiply the score by $M$.
\item Integer penalties, ties, neighbour-set changes and the cutoff remain
discrete. Exponential weighting does not prove global smoothness.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Verification boundary.}
The shipping CLI exposes half/default/double scales $\tau=200,400,800$ while
holding geometry, cutoff, controls and the original comparator fixed.
These are diagnostic choices, not a fitted optimum or a superiority claim.
The existing \texttt{p\_enrichment} is the original radius-one component test;
it does not calibrate $S_X$ or $T_X$.
The present derivation is single-chain. Stable paired joint scoring,
joint calibration and integration of V-loop distances remain separate work.
M3 is outside this derivation.
