\begin{center}
{\Large Distance-local receptor evidence}\par
{\small Historical correspondence, finite ranks and an explicit probability model}
\end{center}
\textbf{Receptors, targets and populations.}
The query $q$ is a TRA, TRB or linked TRA--TRB receptor. Target $X$ includes
peptide and MHC restriction. Its eligible reference set has size $N$;
the declared background has size $M$. A junction includes both conserved anchors.
Full-junction identity exclusion and metadata eligibility precede every count.
References/real controls use distinct junction/V keys; generated controls retain draws.
Those different sampling units do not establish a common null distribution.

\textbf{One additive distance.}
For residues $a,b$, let $s(a,b)$ be BLOSUM62 and set
$c(a,a)=0$, $c(a,b)=\max(0,\min(4,4-s(a,b)))$ for $a\ne b$.
Let $h$ be the junction-length difference, $L$ the longer length,
and $A$ an allowed alignment with one central block of $h$ gaps.
The TCRdist-like distance is
\[
d(q,r)=d_V(q,r)+\min_A\left\{3\sum_{(i,j)\in A}
w_{L,\max(i,j)}c(q_i,r_j)+12h\right\}.
\]
Here $d_V$ sums aligned V-template CDR1/CDR2/CDR2.5 distances, each with weight one;
$i,j$ are zero-based positions in the full junctions. TCRdist3 uses unit interior
weights, trimming three N-terminal and two C-terminal residues. The symmetric
comparison trims three at both ends. Alternatively the shipped empirical profile
uses $u_{L,k}=1-\max\{p_V(k),p_J(L-1-k)\}$ and
$w_{L,k}=u_{L,k}/(L^{-1}\sum_t u_{L,t})$. The profiles $p_V,p_J$ are measured
same-epitope retention probabilities indexed from each end, not exact NDN boundaries.
Native weights have 0.01 resolution/minimum; they weight substitutions.

The term $12h$ is a \emph{linear} gap penalty, not an affine opening/extension
scheme or Smith--Waterman alignment. An affine alternative would be
$g(0)=0$, $g(h)=g_{\rm open}+(h-1)g_{\rm extend}$ for $h>0$; it is a different model.
For linked receptors sum the two chain distances to the \emph{same} reference pair.
Do not infer a joint null from independent marginal control chains.

\textbf{Historical score and the broad-kernel experiment.}
Original vdjmatch sums, over nonexact equal-length neighbours within five substitutions,
\[
S_{\rm old}=\sum_r
\frac{a(q,r)e^{-P(q,r)/400}}
{\max\{N\widehat F_q^{\rm edit}(H(q,r)),0.01\}}.
\]
Here $a$ is the historical V factor, $P$ the positional BLOSUM Gram penalty,
$H$ the substitution count, and $\widehat F_q^{\rm edit}$ the control edit-ball
fraction. Sparse targets additionally use the original marginal V/J/length
log-frequency ratios; historical paired output uses cohort rank fusion.
These contracts remain reproducible and are distinct from the model below.

The preceding experimental score was
$S_{\rm linear}=(W_X/N)/[(W_B+1/2)/(M+1)]$, where
$W_X=\sum_r K_D(d(q,r))$, $W_B=\sum_b K_D(d(q,b))$ and
$K_D(t)=\max(0,1-t/D)$ at fixed radius $D$ (not diameter).
It compares broad average proximity. A very close match may still be diluted.
The new construction restores distance-local normalization; neither experiment
is an accepted replacement default or an established biological TP probability.

\textbf{Background identity matters.}
Generative draws measure recombination accessibility; real controls include
biological selection; VDJdb excluding $X$ reflects other annotated specificities
and ascertainment. Keep these questions separate. Point probability
$P_{\rm gen}(q)$ is not neighbourhood probability. Preserve full junctions for
identity/Pgen even if terminal matching weights are zero.

\textbf{Rank and ties.}
For reference $r_i$, let $R_i$ be its distance rank among $M$ controls plus itself.
Let $H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j$ and $H_0=0$. Define
\[
\boxed{B_X(q)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N h_M(R_i),\qquad
h_M(r)=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}r}.}
\]
This is inverse local background mass with its finite harmonic normalization,
not an exponential kernel with a fitted scale. In the CLI both populations use
integer bins $\lceil d_{\rm native}/200\rceil$. For $l$ controls in lower bins
and $t$ controls in the same bin, average over the possible tied ranks:
\[
\overline h_{l,t}=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}}
\frac{H_{l+t+1}-H_l}{t+1}.
\]
Do not break ties using target labels or input order.

\textbf{Censoring far evidence.}
The fixed radius $D$ determines computation, not a chosen target-dependent gate.
Let $n_D,m_D$ be reference/control counts inside it.
Far references contribute their conditional null mean
\[
\overline h_{\rm far}=\frac{M+1}{H_{M+1}}
\frac{H_{M+1}-H_{m_D}}{M-m_D+1},\qquad
B_X=\frac{\sum_{i:d_i\le D}\overline h_i+(N-n_D)\overline h_{\rm far}}N.
\]
No far ordering enters this statistic. No near evidence on either side gives
$B_X=1$. A singleton target is valid. Empty populations are explicitly unavailable;
the model posterior then remains its prior. No clustering minimum, K cap or
20-residue switch is required.

\textbf{Null bound and Bayesian derivation.}
Under $H_0$, distance labels are exchangeable conditional on the pooled distances
and fixed population sizes. A randomized reference rank is uniform on
$1,\ldots,M+1$, so $\mathbb E_0 h_M(R_i)=1$ and $\mathbb E_0B_X=1$.
Shared controls require no independence between these contributions.
Tie averaging and genuinely censored far ranks preserve this equality by
conditional expectation. Consequently
\[
\boxed{p_{\rm bound}=\min(1,B_X^{-1}),\qquad
\Pr_0(p_{\rm bound}\le\alpha)\le\alpha\quad(0<\alpha<1).}
\]
This Markov bound is conservative, not the exact tail distribution of $B_X$.
A separate nearest-reference test has
$p_{\rm nearest}=\Pr\{K\ge1\}$ for
$K\sim\mathrm{Hypergeom}(N+M,N,m_*+1)$, where $m_*$ counts controls at or below
the nearest reference bin. Upper-bin ties are conservative; censored no-hits
receive one. It is not combined with the full-score bound.

For the \emph{specified rank-tilted alternative} $H_1$, define
$dP_1=B_X\,dP_0$. Its mass is one, so $B_X$ is its Bayes factor.
With declared prior $\pi=\Pr(H_1)$,
\[
\boxed{\Pr(H_1\mid\text{rank evidence})=
\frac{\pi B_X}{1-\pi+\pi B_X}.}
\]
The CLI default $\pi=1/2$ means equal model odds, not biological specificity
prevalence. This alternative is explicitly chosen; its normalization is not
proof that it describes receptor recognition. Deduplication, generated-draw
multiplicity and ascertainment may violate exchangeability. Outputs therefore
remain conditional model quantities until sampling and biological calibration
are validated. P-values are not probability FP, and model posteriors are not
automatically probability TP. Target/route selection requires its own calibration.
General expectation-one evidence: Vovk and Wang, Ann.
Statist.
49:1736--1754 (2021), \href{https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS2020}{doi:10.1214/20-AOS2020}.
Original scoring remains default; M3 stays deferred.
